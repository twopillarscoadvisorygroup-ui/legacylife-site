Cash flow vs. profit: why a profitable business can still run short

By the Legacy Life team · November 4, 2026 · 5 min read

It’s one of the most confusing moments in business: your profit report says you had a good month, but there’s barely enough in the bank to cover payroll. You’re not imagining it. Profit and cash flow are two different things, and knowing the difference can save your business.

Profit is a scorecard. Cash flow is oxygen.

Profit is what’s left after you subtract expenses from the sales you earned in a period. Cash flow is the actual money moving in and out of your bank account, and when it moves.

A business can be profitable on paper and still run out of cash. A business can also have plenty of cash this month while quietly losing money. You need to watch both.

Four common reasons the two don’t match

  1. Customers pay late. You sent the invoice and counted the sale, but the money won’t arrive for 30, 60 or 90 days.
  2. You bought inventory or supplies up front. The cash is gone today, but those items turn into sales later.
  3. Loan payments. Only the interest shows up as an expense, but the full payment leaves your account.
  4. Big one‑time purchases. A new vehicle or piece of equipment can drain cash even though the cost is spread out on your reports.

Quick check: Look at your last three months. Did your bank balance grow by roughly the same amount as your profit? If not, one of the four reasons above is probably why.

How to stay ahead of a cash crunch

  • Make a simple 13‑week cash forecast. List the money you expect in and out each week. It doesn’t need to be fancy to be useful.
  • Tighten your invoicing. Send invoices right away, make paying easy, and follow up on schedule.
  • Build a cushion. Many owners aim to keep a few months of fixed expenses set aside. Start small and grow it.
  • Plan big purchases. Time them for your stronger months, or look at financing before you need it.

The bottom line

Profit tells you if your business model works. Cash flow tells you whether you can keep the doors open while it does. When your books are current, you can see both clearly and make calm decisions instead of stressful ones.
