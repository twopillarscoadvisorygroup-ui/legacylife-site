What is a registered agent, and why does it matter?

By the Legacy Life team · November 16, 2026 · 5 min read

When you form an LLC or corporation, your state asks you to name a registered agent. It’s easy to fill in and forget. But this one detail can make a big difference if something important comes your way.

What a registered agent does

A registered agent is the person or company officially designated to receive legal papers and government notices for your business. That includes:

  • Notice of a lawsuit (“service of process”)
  • State reminders about annual reports and fees
  • Tax and compliance notices from state agencies

The basic requirements

Requirements vary by state, but generally your registered agent must:

  • Have a physical street address in the state where your business is registered (not just a P.O. box)
  • Be available at that address during normal business hours

If your business is registered in more than one state, you typically need a registered agent in each one.

Can I be my own registered agent?

Often, yes, if you meet your state’s requirements. Some owners prefer a professional service because it keeps their home address off public records, makes sure someone is always available and helps keep notices organized.

Watch out: if you move and forget to update your registered agent address, you could miss a lawsuit or a state notice. Missing those can lead to penalties, a judgment you didn’t know about or your business losing good standing.

Quick checklist

  1. Look up your business on your state’s business search website.
  2. Confirm the registered agent name and address are correct.
  3. Update it right away if anything has changed.
  4. Add a yearly reminder to double‑check.
