Before tax season: a data security checklist for small tax offices

By the Legacy Life team · November 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Every filing season, criminals target tax professionals with phishing emails, fake clients and stolen passwords. The IRS’s guide for preparers, Publication 4557, Safeguarding Taxpayer Data, is a great resource. Here’s a practical checklist to work through before your busiest weeks.

Accounts and passwords

  • Turn on multi‑factor authentication for tax software, email, cloud storage and remote access.
  • Give each person their own login. No shared passwords.
  • Remove access for anyone who no longer works with you, including seasonal staff from last year.

Computers and devices

  • Install updates for operating systems, browsers and tax software.
  • Make sure security software is running and current on every device.
  • Encrypt laptops and any drives that hold client data.
  • Set screens to lock automatically when you step away.

Email and phishing

  • Remind staff never to open unexpected attachments or click links asking for passwords.
  • Be cautious of “new clients” who send documents but avoid phone or in‑person contact.
  • Verify any request to change bank details or send data by calling a number you already have.

Backups and paper

  • Back up client data regularly and test that you can actually restore it.
  • Keep paper files in locked cabinets and shred documents you no longer need to keep.

If something goes wrong

  • Know who to call: your IRS Stakeholder Liaison, your state tax agency, your IT provider and your insurer.
  • Keep those contacts in your written incident response plan, not just in someone’s head.

Tip: watch for warning signs like returns rejected because one was already filed under a client’s SSN, more e‑file acknowledgments than you sent, or clients receiving IRS letters about returns they didn’t file. These can signal a data theft.

Put it in writing

This checklist is a great habit. Your WISP is where you make it official, so the right steps happen every season, no matter who’s working the front desk.
