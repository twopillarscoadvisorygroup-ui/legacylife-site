Preparing for your first CARF survey: a 12‑month timeline

By the Legacy Life team · November 16, 2026 · 7 min read

CARF recommends starting preparation about 12 months before your desired survey date. That may sound generous, but policies need to be written, approved, shared with staff and actually in use before surveyors arrive. Here’s a practical timeline.

Months 12 to 10: Get your bearings

  • Get the current CARF standards manual for your program area.
  • Choose an internal accreditation lead and a small working team.
  • Do a gap analysis: compare what you have today with each applicable standard.
  • Pick a target survey window.

Months 9 to 6: Build the documents

  • Write or update policies and procedures, organized by standard.
  • Develop required written plans, such as risk management, accessibility, technology, health and safety, cultural competency and performance improvement.
  • Set up your outcomes and performance measurement process so you have real data by survey time.
  • Get leadership and board approval where required.

Months 5 to 3: Put it into practice

  • Train staff on new and updated policies, and document the training.
  • Organize personnel files, credentials and training records.
  • Start using new forms and processes so there’s a track record surveyors can see.
  • Submit your survey application on the timeline CARF requires.

Months 2 to 1: Rehearse

  • Hold a mock survey: review records, walk the facility and practice interviews.
  • Fix anything the mock survey uncovers.
  • Build an easy‑to‑navigate evidence binder or digital folder that maps documents to standards.

Survey and after

  • Keep your team calm and prepared: surveyors want to see how you really work.
  • After the report arrives, write your Quality Improvement Plan to address any recommendations.
  • Set a calendar to review policies and plans regularly so you stay ready.

Biggest lesson: the programs that struggle usually didn’t lack effort. They ran out of time to put new policies into daily practice. Start early.
