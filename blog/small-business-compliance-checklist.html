The small‑business compliance checklist: 8 things to keep current

By the Legacy Life team · November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

“Compliance” sounds intimidating, but for most small businesses it comes down to a short list of things to keep current. Miss one, and your business can fall out of good standing with your state, which can lead to penalties, trouble getting loans or even losing the protection your entity gives you.

Keep in mind: Rules and deadlines differ by state, city and industry. Use this list as a starting point and check the exact requirements where your business is registered.

The checklist

  1. Annual or biennial report. Most states require LLCs and corporations to file a regular report and pay a fee. This is the most commonly missed filing.
  2. Registered agent. Your business needs a registered agent with a current address on file to receive official notices.
  3. Business licenses and permits. Many cities, counties and states require general or industry‑specific licenses that must be renewed.
  4. EIN and accurate records with the IRS. Keep your business information up to date if your address or responsible party changes.
  5. Separate business bank account. Mixing personal and business money makes bookkeeping harder and can weaken your liability protection.
  6. Operating agreement or bylaws. Keep your governing documents current, especially when owners or roles change.
  7. Meeting records, if required. Corporations often need to hold and document certain meetings. Know what applies to you.
  8. Organized financial records. Clean, monthly books make every other filing easier and keep you ready for lenders and your tax professional.

Make it automatic

  • Create a compliance calendar with every due date for the year, plus reminders 30 days ahead.
  • Keep documents in one place: formation papers, EIN letter, licenses, agreements and past filings.
  • Check your good standing with your state once a year. It usually takes just a few minutes online.

The bottom line

Compliance isn’t about paperwork for its own sake. It protects what you’ve built. A simple system and a yearly check‑in are usually all it takes to stay on track.
