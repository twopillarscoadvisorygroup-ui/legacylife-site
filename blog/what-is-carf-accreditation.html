What is CARF accreditation, and is it right for your program?

By the Legacy Life team · November 16, 2026 · 6 min read

If you run a behavioral health, substance use or community services program, you’ve probably heard of CARF. Many payers, referral partners and state agencies look for it. Here’s a plain‑language overview.

What CARF is

CARF International is an independent, nonprofit accrediting body for health and human services providers. Programs that earn CARF accreditation have shown, through an on‑site review, that they meet CARF’s published standards for quality, safety and good business practice.

Why programs pursue it

  • Funding and contracts. Some payers and state contracts require or prefer accredited providers.
  • Referrals and trust. Accreditation signals to clients, families and partners that your program meets recognized standards.
  • Better operations. The process pushes you to document how you work, measure outcomes and improve.

How the process works

  1. Learn the standards. CARF publishes standards manuals by program area. You work from the current manual for your services.
  2. Prepare. Bring your policies, plans, records and practices in line with the standards. This is usually the biggest step.
  3. Apply for a survey. You request a survey and CARF schedules surveyors.
  4. The survey. Surveyors who are experienced professionals in the field visit, review documents, observe services and interview leaders, staff and people served.
  5. The decision. CARF issues an accreditation decision and a written report.
  6. Ongoing improvement. You respond to any recommendations with a Quality Improvement Plan and keep meeting the standards between surveys.

What surveyors look for: not just that policies exist, but that they are approved, known by staff and actually followed. The paperwork and the practice need to match.

Is it right for you?

CARF accreditation is a real commitment of time and attention. It tends to make the most sense when funders or partners require it, when you’re growing and want stronger systems or when you want an outside measure of quality. Starting early, about a year before your target survey date, makes the process far more manageable.
