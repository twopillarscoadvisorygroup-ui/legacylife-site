Personal vs. business credit: what lenders actually look at

By the Legacy Life team · November 7, 2026 · 6 min read

When you apply for a business loan or line of credit, many owners are surprised to learn the lender looks at two credit pictures: yours personally, and your business’s. Here’s how they fit together.

Your personal credit

For newer and smaller businesses, lenders often lean heavily on the owner’s personal credit score and history. It tells them how you’ve handled borrowing in the past. Late payments, high card balances and recent collections can all make approval harder or borrowing more expensive.

Your business credit

Your business can build its own credit history, separate from you. Business credit bureaus track things like whether the business pays vendors and lenders on time. Over time, a strong business profile can help your company qualify on its own merits.

Good to know: To build business credit, the business generally needs to look and act like a separate business: its own legal entity, its own EIN, its own bank account, and accounts in the business’s name.

What lenders commonly review

  • Credit history, both personal and business
  • Time in business. Many lenders prefer at least two years.
  • Revenue and cash flow, and whether you can comfortably make the payment
  • Existing debt compared with your income
  • Up‑to‑date financial statements and bank records

Every lender sets its own rules, so requirements vary. But organized records and a clean credit history help almost everywhere.

Five steps you can start this month

  1. Pull your personal credit reports and check them for errors.
  2. Make sure your business is formed properly and has its own EIN and bank account.
  3. Open a few vendor or supplier accounts in the business’s name and pay them on time.
  4. Keep credit card balances well below their limits.
  5. Get your books current so you can produce financial statements quickly.

The bottom line

Strong personal credit opens the first doors. Strong business credit opens bigger ones and helps keep your personal finances separate. Building both takes time, so the best moment to start is well before you need the money.
